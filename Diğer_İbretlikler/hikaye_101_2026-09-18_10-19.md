Start with the map above, then compare the actual driving route. A station that looks close can take longer to reach because of divided highways, one-way streets, limited exits, construction, or a difficult entrance.

If your fuel level is very low, prioritize a safe station you can reach. If you have more range, compare distance, listed hours, fuel type, and price before choosing.

Use the map listing to check current hours, especially late at night, early in the morning, on holidays, or during severe weather.

Open now and open 24 hours are not the same thing. A station can be open at the moment without operating all day and night. A location that normally runs 24 hours can also close temporarily.

If you regularly drive overnight, use our 24-hour gas station guide and still confirm the individual location before relying on it.

Use your current location, then compare the nearest map results by driving distance.

If nothing suitable appears within about one mile, widen your search to 3, 5, or 10 miles instead of driving around hoping to spot a station.

A station half a mile away may require a long U-turn or highway exit, while a station one mile away may sit directly on your route.

Compare the price per gallon with the extra distance, the number of gallons you need, traffic, and any membership or payment requirement.

If another station is 10 cents cheaper and you need 10 gallons, the maximum pump difference is $1. Driving several extra miles may use part of that saving and cost additional time.

If one station is 8 cents cheaper and you need 8 gallons, the difference is only 64 cents.

With the same 8-cent difference, a 25-gallon fill-up changes the pump difference to $2, so price comparison matters more.

Before choosing a station, make sure it carries the fuel your vehicle actually needs.

Use the octane grade required or recommended by your vehicle manufacturer. A higher octane number does not automatically make premium the better choice for every car.

Not every station sells diesel. Confirm pump availability before driving over, especially if you are in a diesel pickup, van, RV, or commercial vehicle.

E85 is intended for compatible flex-fuel vehicles. Check your fuel door, owner’s manual, or manufacturer guidance before using it.

The U.S. Department of Energy provides an Alternative Fueling Station Locator for E85 and other alternative fuels.

A brand search can help when you use a rewards program, membership, preferred payment method, or familiar fuel network.

Do not assume every location under the same brand has identical hours, services, or fuel types. Check the individual station before making a special trip.

Air-pump availability and fees vary by location, and a machine can be temporarily out of service.

If you need tire air, search specifically for an air pump and confirm the individual location before driving over. If a tire is losing pressure quickly, visibly damaged, or seriously underinflated, adding air may not solve the problem.

A restroom icon on a listing does not always mean the restroom will be available when you arrive.

Before making a special stop, check whether the convenience store is open, whether the restroom is public, and whether the station is easy to leave and re-enter from your route. Some pumps operate after the attached store closes, while the restroom remains inside the locked store.

For a family trip or late-night drive, a slightly farther location with an open store and confirmed restroom may be more useful than the absolute closest pump.

If you want to fuel and wash the car in one stop, compare more than a simple “car wash” label.

Look for restrictions if you have roof racks, bike racks, oversized tires, low ground clearance, aftermarket accessories, extra height, or loose items in a pickup bed.

Also compare the total wash-and-fuel cost instead of assuming a bundled discount is automatically cheaper.

A convenience store matters more when your stop needs to solve more than one problem. If you are planning a proper meal during your journey, check the latest Olive Garden menu with prices before choosing where to stop.

Do not assume the store and pumps follow the same hours. If you need something inside, check the store hours rather than only whether the fuel pumps are operating.

A standard neighborhood station may be awkward for a larger vehicle even when it sells the right fuel.

Look for diesel where needed, wide entrances, larger fueling lanes, enough turning space, high-clearance access, and truck or RV parking where available. On interstate trips, a station that sits directly on your route may be more useful than the station with the shortest straight-line distance.

A station can be physically close but still be difficult or unsafe to reach on foot because of highways, missing sidewalks, barriers, or limited pedestrian crossings. Use walking directions rather than relying only on driving distance.

Plan fuel stops before the tank is almost empty, especially outside major cities.

Check your remaining fuel range, distance to the next town, station hours, fuel type, weather, highway exits, and road closures. In rural areas, the gap between suitable stations can be much greater than in a city.

If you are unsure when the next usable station will appear, filling earlier is usually more practical than waiting for the lowest price.

Before relying on a listing, check the address, opening hours, fuel type, recent price timestamp if one is shown, and directions. Holiday hours can differ from normal weekly hours, and a reported fuel price may change later the same day.

For an important stop, especially late at night or far from populated areas, verify the station before you commit to a long detour.
