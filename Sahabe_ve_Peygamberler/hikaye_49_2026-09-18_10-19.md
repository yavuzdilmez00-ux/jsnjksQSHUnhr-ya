FSMA requires written records of preventive control monitoring, corrective actions, and verification activities for food safety preventive controls that include equipment maintenance as a control measure. Maintenance records for equipment involved in process controls — temperature-controlled cooking, pasteurization, metal detection — must demonstrate that the equipment was maintained in a condition that supports the food safety function it performs. When maintenance activities reveal equipment conditions that could compromise food safety — a failed temperature probe on a pasteurizer, a damaged food-contact surface on a filling line, a metal detector that fails calibration — FSMA requires documented corrective action records that identify the problem, describe the corrective action taken, evaluate affected product for safety, and prevent recurrence. A food safety CMMS must generate corrective action work orders that capture all required data elements in a format that satisfies FDA records review.

Allergen changeover cleaning requires verification steps — visual inspection, ATP swabbing, allergen-specific swab testing — that must be documented and signed off before the production line can be released for the next allergen profile. A food-grade CMMS must enforce allergen changeover sanitation workflows with mandatory verification capture and block work order completion until all required verification steps are recorded, preventing inadvertent allergen cross-contact through incomplete sanitation.

Cleaning-in-place systems in food and beverage facilities require regular maintenance of spray devices, chemical dosing systems, heat exchangers, and flow controls — with validation records that demonstrate CIP cycles deliver the required time, temperature, chemical concentration, and flow velocity parameters at food-contact surfaces. Maintenance records for CIP components must be linked to CIP validation data in a CMMS that tracks both equipment condition and process performance.

Pre-operational sanitation inspection records — the daily verification that food-contact equipment is clean and ready for production — are among the most frequently requested records during FDA inspections and third-party food safety certification audits.
