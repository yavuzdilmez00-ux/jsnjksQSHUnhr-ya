SAP's BDC story has Datasphere as the governed data layer, SAC for reporting, and Databricks for predictive ML. Insight and prediction use cases get all the attention. The third — the most valuable — barely gets mentioned.

Your SAC dashboard is a mirror. It shows what already happened.Your Databricks ML is a crystal ball. It predicts what's likely to happen. Neither one is a brain.

A brain decides. How much risk to accept for a specific reward? Which decisions must be made now despite uncertainty, which ones can be deferred to preserve optionality? How to price to move excess inventory nearing shelf-life expiry? That's prescriptive analytics in the AI age — stochastic optimization under uncertainty. Platforms like VYAN — supply-driven demand shaping, profitable-to-promise, service / margin / cash tradeoffs — are purpose-built for this.

But real-time S/4HANA operational data is the oxygen the brain runs on. Feed it stale inputs and even the most sophisticated engine suffocates. Getting synchronous high volume feeds out of S/4HANA reliably at needed latency and acceptable cost? That's the gap. BDC can solve this. The conversation just hasn't caught up yet.

Most BDC discussion focuses on ingestion: replicate data, build a semantic layer, feed the mirror. But there's an equally powerful use case: consumption — serving governed, calculated S/4 data outbound to the brain with business logic intact and freshness in seconds, not hours.

The timing is right. SAP is phasing out HANA sidecar architectures, with compliance deadlines hitting in 2027. The sidecar outbound CDC struggles — timestamp-based delta handling across tables updating at different cadences create integrity gaps. A PO changes, accounting hasn't caught up, systems diverge. Sidecar customers need a reliable replacement .

The pattern we're designing in the field: BDC + CAP. BDC as the governed calculation layer — sidecar logic moves into managed data products on HANA Cloud. Margins, inventory, pricing — governed in one place, not reconstructed everywhere. BTP CAP as the serving layer — OData and REST endpoints giving the brain API-driven access at near-real-time freshness with consistent, governed answers, not just raw data. Calculate once in BDC. Serve many through CAP. The mirror, the crystal ball, and the brain — all getting the same answer.

Databricks excels at the crystal ball work. But it isn't designed to help make better decisions at speed and scale - to optimize your P&L and cashflow under uncertainty. That's a Decision Intelligence platform such as VYAN. 

BDC holds data products. BTP-CAP serves them. VYAN and similar platforms turn them into decisions. If your S/4 data powers pricing, promotions, supply chain, or allocation decisions downstream — map where it's needed, how it gets there, and what's breaking across the enterprise. The mirror and the crystal ball are well-served. It's time to feed the brain.

❗Warning for anyone using #Databricks Online Tables / Feature Serving

Databricks is decommissioning online tables. Going forward, the recommended approach is to use the Databricks Online Feature Store (which uses Lakebase) and publish feature tables to an online store.

This change has significant cost implications. We noticed it firsthand while teaching our last MLOps with Databricks cohort: same lab materials, smallest possible online-store instances, yet our “Database” costs spiked.

Originally, we let every student create their own online store. In hindsight, it’s more cost-efficient to reuse online stores whenever possible rather than creating new ones per project.

If you’re planning labs, demos, or production workloads, keep this in mind!

🚨 Power BI Deployment Gotcha – A Lesson Learned the Hard Way 🚨

Recently, I faced a puzzling issue while working on a Power BI report:
✅ Successfully deployed to Stage
 ❌ Production deployment option was missing

At first glance, everything looked fine — no errors, no warnings. But after digging deeper, we uncovered the real culprit 👇

🔍 Root Cause
The report was:
Downloaded from Power BI Service
Copied to SharePoint for version control
Then reused for deployment

This process silently added extra sensitivity restrictions to the PBIX file, limiting its deployment to the same environment only. As a result, the Production deployment option disappeared — without any clear indication from Power BI.

💡 Key Lessons
PBIX files retain metadata when downloaded from the Power BI Service
Sensitivity labels & tenant-level restrictions travel with the file
Manual version control via SharePoint can unintentionally break CI/CD flows

✅ Recommended Solutions
 ✔️ Use Deployment Pipelines for environment promotion
 ✔️ Maintain version control via Power BI Service + Git integration (Fabric)
 ✔️ If PBIX reuse is unavoidable:
        Re-publish the report from Power BI Desktop
        Validate Sensitivity Labels before deployment
        ✔️ Establish a clear governance rule:
        👉 Never redeploy PBIX files downloaded from Service across environments

📌 Final Thought
Sometimes the hardest Power BI issues aren’t DAX or performance —
 they’re governance, metadata, and invisible platform rules.
